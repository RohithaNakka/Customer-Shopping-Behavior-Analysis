Project Overview
This project analyses a customer shopping dataset containing 3,900 purchase records and 18 columns. It combines customer demographics, product information, purchase amounts, seasonal preferences, review ratings, discount usage, shipping types, subscription status, and purchase history.

The workflow covers data preparation in Python, business analysis in PostgreSQL, and interactive reporting in Power BI. The objective is to turn the supplied purchase records into descriptive insights that can inform product, promotion, and customer-engagement decisions.


Business Problem
How can a retail company leverage customer shopping data to identify purchasing trends, improve customer engagement, and optimize marketing and product strategies?
Project Objectives

- Inspect the structure and quality of the dataset.
- Clean and standardize fields using Python and Pandas.
- Create analytical features such as age groups and an approximate purchase-frequency interval.
- Use SQL to investigate customer spending, products, discounts, shipping, and subscription behaviour.
- Build an interactive Power BI dashboard with KPI cards, charts, and slicers.
- Communicate recommendations supported by validated analysis.

Project Workflow
1. Data loading and exploration
- Loaded the source dataset into a Pandas DataFrame.
- Reviewed dimensions, data types, non-null counts, descriptive statistics, and missing values.
- Inspected categorical fields and potential redundancies.
2. Data cleaning and preparation
- Renamed columns to lowercase snake_case.
- Identified 37 missing values in review_rating.
- Imputed missing ratings using the median rating within the corresponding product category, as described in the analysis workflow.
- Created age_group by binning customer ages. See the notebook for the exact bin boundaries and labels.
- Created purchase_frequency_days by mapping the categorical purchase-frequency field to an approximate number of days. This is a derived approximation, not a calculation from transaction dates.
- Compared discount_applied and promo_code_used. Exclude promo_code_used only after confirming that both columns match for every record.
3. PostgreSQL integration
- Connected the prepared DataFrame to PostgreSQL.
- Loaded the cleaned data into a database table.
- Used aggregations, filters, conditional expressions, subqueries, CTEs, and window functions to investigate the business questions.
4. Power BI reporting
- Created KPI cards for customer count, revenue, average purchase amount, and average review rating.
- Added slicers for category, season, subscription status, and location.
- Built charts comparing category revenue, subscription status, gender contribution, age-group spending, and seasonal revenue.
Business Questions
#	Business question	Analysis
1	How much revenue is generated by male and female customers?	Sum purchase amount by gender.
2	Which discount users spent more than the overall average purchase amount?	Filter discount users and compare purchase amount with the overall average.
3	Which five products have the highest average review rating?	Calculate average rating by product and rank descending.
4	How does average purchase amount compare between Standard and Express shipping?	Compare average purchase amount across the two shipping types.
5	How do subscribers and non-subscribers compare?	Compare record count, average purchase amount, and total revenue.
6	Which products have the highest percentage of discounted purchases?	Calculate discounted records as a percentage of all records per product.
7	How are records distributed across purchase-history groups?	Group records using previous_purchases bands.
8	What are the top purchased products within each category?	Count product records and rank products within each category.
9	Are customers with more than five previous purchases more likely to subscribe?	Compare subscription rates across purchase-history groups.
10	Which age groups contribute the most revenue?	Sum purchase amount by age_group.

Potential Business Recommendations
Treat these as areas to investigate. Convert them into final recommendations only after confirming the actual SQL results and dashboard values.
1. Subscription engagement: Compare subscriber and non-subscriber spending and revenue before proposing membership benefits.
2. Customer retention: Examine purchase-history groups and subscription rates to identify groups that may respond to loyalty initiatives.
3. Discount strategy: Review products with high discount-use rates. Profitability cannot be established without product cost or margin data.
4. Product positioning: Consider purchase volume and average review ratings together when identifying products for promotion.
5. Targeted marketing: Use observed differences across age groups, gender, locations, and product categories to inform campaign hypotheses.
6. Customer experience: Compare shipping-type purchase amounts and review ratings to identify areas for further investigation.



Insight-writing approach: Observation → Business implication → Recommended action. Include the actual metric and comparison from the validated analysis. Do not infer causation from descriptive results.
